## The six-layer stack

Each domain file climbs the stack from the bottom up:

```
         FEATURES         ← what the material + technique gives you
            ↑
         TECHNIQUE        ← how the material is found, identified, processed or treated
            ↑
         MATERIAL         ← what carries the property
            ↑
         DISCOVERY        ← the mineral, effect or law itself
            ↑
         MEASUREMENT      ← the instrument that first saw it
            ↑
         VOCABULARY       ← the terms that let you reason about all of the above
```

## The eight piles on the bench

Every mineral question is one of eight things, or a combination. Lay them out on a tray:

1. Something for [mineralogy](01-mineralogy/README.md) — quartz, calcite, pyrite, halite, a mica flake.
2. Something for [crystallography](02-crystallography/README.md) — a well-formed crystal: a salt cube, a quartz point, an alum crystal you grew.
3. Something for [geochemistry](03-geochemistry/README.md) — a bottle of mineral water with its ion list, sea salt, a rusty nail.
4. Something for [petrology](04-petrology/README.md) — granite, basalt, sandstone, marble, slate.
5. Something for [economic geology](05-economic-geology/README.md) — a piece of ore: galena, chalcopyrite or magnetite sand.
6. Something for [gemology](06-gemology/README.md) — an amethyst, a cubic zirconia, a glass imitation, a loupe.
7. Something for [materials science](07-materials-science/README.md) — a quartz watch crystal, a ceramic tile, plaster of Paris.
8. Something for [environmental mineralogy](08-environmental-mineralogy/README.md) — bentonite cat litter, an orange iron-stained pebble, garden soil.

Most of these cost little at a rock shop or come from a beach, a garden or a kitchen. This is the real curriculum. Everything that follows is a map through what's on the tray.

## How to work through a pile

For each pile:

1. Pick one specimen from the tray.
2. Look at it with the loupe. Draw what you see on paper.
3. Name what you can. Just name it. Quartz. Feldspar. Rust. Clay. If you don't know, scratch it, streak it, weigh it — the mineral tells you about itself.
4. Figure out what it records or does. Slow cooling. Hot fluid. Weathering. Brilliance. Piezoelectricity. Sorption.
5. Run a kitchen-table version of the test or process. It doesn't have to be precise. It has to work.
6. Measure it. Scale, ruler, phone camera, red-cabbage pH. Compare with published values.
7. Then look up the equation. Now it means something.

If you can do steps 1 through 6 for every pile, you know this discipline better than most people who only read about it.

## Eight Domains at a Glance

| Domain | What it studies | Typical scale | First tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mineralogy | Mineral composition, properties, classification, and formation | Grain (µm) to hand specimen (cm); formation in hours (evaporites) to millions of years (plutons) | Hand lens |
| Crystallography | Atomic arrangement, symmetry, and crystal structure | Ångström unit cells; crystals from nanometres to metres; growth in seconds to millions of years | Contact and reflecting goniometers |
| Geochemistry | Distribution and movement of chemical elements | ppb to wt%; atoms to planets; decay clocks from days to billions of years | Spectroscopes |
| Petrology | Rocks and their constituent minerals | Grains (mm) to mountain belts (1000 km); lava quenches in seconds, plutons cool over 10⁴–10⁶ years | Field mapping |
| Economic geology | Deposits, ores, and resource formation | Deposits from tens of metres to kilometres; mines last decades; deposits form over 10⁴–10⁶ years | Geological maps |
| Gemology | Jewelry materials, identification, treatments, quality, and care | Inclusions of micrometres; stones of millimetres; a carat is 0.2 g | Loupe |
| Materials science | Physical properties and technological applications | Atomic bonds to engineered parts; processing in seconds (quenching) to weeks (crystal growth) | Furnaces |
| Environmental mineralogy | Weathering, contamination, and mineral–environment interactions | Nanoparticles and mineral surfaces to watersheds; reactions in hours (sorption) to millennia (soil formation) | pH and Eh meters |
