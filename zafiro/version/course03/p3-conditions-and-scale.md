> Pressure, temperature and fluid composition decide what forms and what survives. Scale — atoms to mountain belts, seconds to billions of years — decides which tool can see it.

## Conditions: where things form

Typical ranges; real settings vary widely.

| Setting | Temperature | Pressure / depth | Fluid | What forms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Earth's surface | ~0–40 °C | ~0.1 MPa | Rain, groundwater, O₂, CO₂, microbes | Clays, iron oxides, carbonates, evaporites |
| Diagenesis | up to ~200 °C | a few km of burial | Pore water | Cements: quartz, calcite, clays |
| Hydrothermal veins | ~150–400 °C | upper crust | Hot saline water | Quartz, sulfides, gold |
| Granite magma | ~650–850 °C | ~5–30 km | Melt with water | Quartz, feldspars, micas; pegmatites |
| Basalt lava | ~1100–1200 °C | surface | Melt | Plagioclase, pyroxene, olivine, glass |
| Regional metamorphism | ~300–750 °C | ~0.2–1.2 GPa | Little, released by reactions | Garnet, staurolite, kyanite, sillimanite |
| Diamond source | ~900–1300 °C | > ~4.5 GPa, > ~140 km | Mantle melts and fluids | Diamond |

The laboratory copies these on purpose: autoclaves for quartz ([Materials science](07-materials-science/README.md)), presses for diamond ([Gemology](06-gemology/README.md)), kilns for cement and ceramics.

## Equilibrium and rate

- **Equilibrium** tells you what *should* form at given conditions (phase diagrams, Eh–pH diagrams).
- **Rate** tells you what *actually* survives — why diamonds, glasses and aragonite shells exist at the surface at all.
- Most questions in these courses need both: where something formed (equilibrium) and how much of it is left (rate).
