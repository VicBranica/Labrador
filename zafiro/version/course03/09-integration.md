You've looked at eight piles. Real specimens and real products live between them.

A **copper wire** = a porphyry deposit (economic geology) of chalcopyrite (mineralogy) with a known structure (crystallography), made by metal-rich fluids from a magma (geochemistry, petrology), mined and floated and smelted (economic geology, materials science), leaving pyrite-bearing tailings (environmental mineralogy). Malachite from the oxidized zone may end up as a bead (gemology).

A **sapphire ring** = corundum (mineralogy) with Fe and Ti in a trigonal structure (crystallography, geochemistry), from a metamorphic or basaltic source (petrology), washed into a placer and mined (economic geology), heat-treated and cut (gemology, materials science), with mining and treatment footprints to disclose (environmental mineralogy).

## How to read a specimen

1. Start with what you can see: colour, luster, shape, grain size. Photograph it.
2. Pick two independent properties and measure them — SG and hardness, or RI and SG for a gem.
3. Name the mineral or rock. If two names fit, find a third property.
4. Ask where it formed: which rock, which conditions (Pillar 3).
5. Ask which processes brought it here (Pillar 1).
6. Ask what it is worth and to whom: ore, gem, material, or none.
7. Ask what it does in air and water: inert, dissolving, acid-forming, fibrous.
8. Write it down — specimen number, locality, observations, conclusion.

## Field and lab heuristics

1. Look before you test; non-destructive before destructive.
2. Colour lies; streak, hardness and SG don't.
3. Two independent properties before you name anything.
4. A specimen without a locality has lost half its story.
5. Match the tool to the scale of the question.
6. Equilibrium says what should form; rate says what survived.
7. Grade × tonnage × recovery decides ore — and the same numbers size the waste.
8. Every mine is also a future waste site; plan for both.
9. Disclose treatments and synthetics, always.
10. Dust, fibres and acids are the real hazards; at home, vinegar is the strongest acid you need.
