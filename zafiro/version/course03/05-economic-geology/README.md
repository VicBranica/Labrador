## A. What you're looking at

Copper wire. Aluminium foil. A phone battery (lithium, cobalt, graphite). Table salt from an evaporite mine. A gold ring. A bag of gravel — by tonnage, the largest mined commodity.

## B. The one thing to understand

An ore deposit is an ordinary element concentrated far above its crustal average by a geological process, in a place and at a grade where it pays to dig it out. Geology makes the concentration; economics decides whether it is ore. The same rock can be ore at one metal price and waste at another.

**Key relations:** Concentration factor = ore grade / average crustal abundance · Contained metal = tonnage × grade; recovered metal = contained × recovery · 1 g/t = 1 ppm

## 1. Vocabulary

*The terms that let you reason about everything below.*

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ore | Rock from which a mineral or metal can be extracted at a profit. |
| Gangue | The worthless minerals in an ore. |
| Grade | Concentration of the valuable component — % for base metals, g/t for precious metals. |
| Cut-off grade | The lowest grade that is mined as ore rather than sent to waste. |
| Tonnage | Mass of ore in a deposit, usually in million tonnes (Mt). |
| Concentration factor | How many times above crustal average the deposit grade is. |
| Resource / reserve | Resource: estimated quantity with reasonable prospects (inferred, indicated, measured). Reserve: the economically mineable part (probable, proven). |
| Deposit type | Porphyry, volcanogenic massive sulfide (VMS), sediment-hosted, orogenic gold, placer, laterite, evaporite, kimberlite, pegmatite. |
| Hydrothermal fluid | Hot water that dissolves, carries and deposits metals. |
| Alteration halo | The zone of chemically changed rock around a deposit — often bigger than the ore and easier to find. |
| Beneficiation | Upgrading ore before smelting — crushing, grinding, flotation, gravity and magnetic separation. |
| Froth flotation | Separating minerals by making the valuable ones attach to air bubbles. |
| Tailings | Finely ground waste left after processing. |

## 4. Material

*What carries the property.*

- **Copper:** chalcopyrite CuFeS₂ (the main ore mineral), bornite, chalcocite; malachite and azurite near the surface.
- **Lead and zinc:** galena PbS and sphalerite ZnS.
- **Iron:** hematite and magnetite, mostly from banded iron formations.
- **Aluminium:** bauxite — a lateritic mix of gibbsite and other aluminium hydroxides.
- **Tin, tungsten:** cassiterite SnO₂, wolframite, scheelite.
- **Gold:** native gold, often invisible in pyrite and arsenopyrite.
- **Lithium:** spodumene in pegmatites; lithium-rich brines.
- **Diamonds:** kimberlite and lamproite pipes; alluvial placers.

## 5. Technique

*How the material is found, identified, processed or treated.*

- **Exploration** — mapping, soil and stream sampling, geophysics (magnetics, gravity, induced polarization), drilling.
- **Resource estimation** — drill-hole assays interpolated into a block model; reported under codes such as JORC (first edition 1989) or NI 43-101 (2001).
- **Comminution** — crushing and grinding to free ore grains from gangue — the largest energy cost of most mines.
- **Froth flotation** — the workhorse for sulfide ores.
- **Leaching** — heap leaching and cyanidation for gold; acid leaching for copper oxides.
- **Smelting and refining** — from concentrate to metal.

### The clever trick

**Froth flotation.** Grind the ore, add a collector chemical that coats only the sulfide grains and makes them water-repellent, then blow air through the slurry. The sulfides ride the bubbles into a froth that is skimmed off; the silicate gangue sinks. Flotation made huge low-grade deposits worth mining and is still how most of the world's copper, lead and zinc is concentrated.

### Signature techniques

Diamond-core drilling; assaying; block modelling; comminution; froth flotation; heap leaching.

## 6. Features

*What the material + technique combination gives you.*

| Function | Material | Technique | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find a deposit | Alteration halo, geochemical anomaly | Mapping, sampling, geophysics | Targets for drilling |
| Measure a deposit | Drill core | Assays, block model | Tonnage and grade with confidence levels |
| Concentrate sulfides | Chalcopyrite, galena, sphalerite | Froth flotation | Concentrate of ~25–30% Cu from <1% ore |
| Recover gold | Low-grade oxidized ore | Heap leach, cyanidation | Economic at ~1 g/t |
| Separate heavy minerals | Placer sands | Gravity (jigs, spirals), magnets | Cheap, no chemicals |

Deposit families: magmatic (chromite, PGE, Ni sulfide) · porphyry · VMS · sediment-hosted · orogenic gold · epithermal · placer · laterite · evaporite · kimberlite · pegmatite.

## D. What to build

1. Pan for "gold": mix sand with a few small lead-free fishing weights or steel shot in a pie dish of water and pan until only the heavy pieces stay. Density does the work, as in every placer.
2. Magnetic separation: drag a magnet in a plastic bag through dark beach sand. The black grains that jump are magnetite — an iron ore.
3. A flotation demonstration: stir ground charcoal and sand into water with a drop of dish soap, then bubble air through with a straw or aquarium pump. The charcoal rides the froth; the sand sinks.

## E. The example everyone should work

A porphyry copper deposit holds 500 Mt of ore at 0.5% Cu. Average crustal copper is a few tens of ppm (published estimates differ; take 50 ppm). Plant recovery is 85%.

1. Grade in ppm: 0.5% = 5000 ppm. Concentration factor = 5000 / 50 = 100×.
2. Contained copper = 500 Mt × 0.005 = 2.5 Mt Cu.
3. Recovered copper = 2.5 × 0.85 = 2.1 Mt Cu.
4. Waste: almost all of the 500 Mt ends up as tailings — about 235 t of tailings per tonne of copper recovered (500 Mt ÷ 2.1 Mt).
5. Gold for comparison: crust a few ppb (take 4 ppb); ore at 2 g/t = 2000 ppb → concentration factor 500×.

Grade × tonnage × recovery is the whole business in one line — and the same line says how much waste the environmental pile inherits.
